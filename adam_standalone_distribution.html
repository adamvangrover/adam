The Adam Algorithm Specification

Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation) combines the advantages of two popular methods: AdaGrad (which works well with sparse gradients) and RMSprop (which works well in on-line and non-stationary settings).

1. Gradient Computation at timestep $t$:
$g_t \leftarrow \nabla_\theta f_t(\theta_{t-1})$
2. Biased 1st Moment Vector (Exponential Moving Average of Gradients):
$m_t \leftarrow \beta_1 \cdot m_{t-1} + (1 - \beta_1) \cdot g_t$
3. Biased 2nd Raw Moment Vector (Exponential Moving Average of Squared Gradients):
$v_t \leftarrow \beta_2 \cdot v_{t-1} + (1 - \beta_2) \cdot g_t^2$
4. Bias Corrections for Initialization Zero-Vector Attenuation:
$\hat{m}_t \leftarrow \frac{m_t}{1 - \beta_1^t}, \quad \hat{v}_t \leftarrow \frac{v_t}{1 - \beta_2^t}$
5. Parameter Update Step:
$\theta_t \leftarrow \theta_{t-1} - \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} \cdot \hat{m}_t$

Why Bias Correction Matters: Theoretical Proof

Assume $m_0 = 0$. Unrolling the recurrence relation for $m_t$ gives:

$$m_t = (1 - \beta_1) \sum_{i=1}^t \beta_1^{t-i} g_i$$
Taking expectations on both sides (assuming true first moment $\mathbb{E}[g_i] = \mathbb{E}[g_t]$):
$$\mathbb{E}[m_t] = \mathbb{E}\left[(1 - \beta_1) \sum_{i=1}^t \beta_1^{t-i} g_i\right] = \mathbb{E}[g_t] \cdot (1 - \beta_1) \sum_{i=1}^t \beta_1^{t-i} = \mathbb{E}[g_t] \cdot (1 - \beta_1^t)$$
Therefore, dividing by $(1 - \beta_1^t)$ yields an unbiased estimator: $\mathbb{E}[\hat{m}_t] = \mathbb{E}[g_t]$.

Adam vs. AdamW (Decoupled Weight Decay)

In L2 regularization within standard Adam, weight decay is applied as part of the gradient: $g_t \leftarrow \nabla f_t(\theta) + \lambda \theta$. Consequently, weights with larger historical gradients receive smaller relative decay. AdamW decouples the decay from gradient updates: $\theta_t \leftarrow \theta_{t-1} - \eta_t \lambda \theta_{t-1} - \frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\hat{v}_t} + \epsilon} \hat{m}_t$, restoring true weight regularization.
