Executive Summary

Global markets continue to operate under a high cost-of-capital regime characterized by elevated sovereign yields, significant AI infrastructure investment, resilient—but increasingly selective—credit markets, and persistent geopolitical uncertainty surrounding energy supply. Institutional capital is rotating away from duration-sensitive, narrative-driven growth assets toward businesses with tangible cash generation, pricing power, and critical infrastructure exposure.

Our base case remains constructive but cautious: economic activity slows without a systemic recession, liquidity remains functional, and capital allocation becomes increasingly discriminating. The primary risks remain an energy supply shock, renewed inflation pressure, and a deterioration in corporate refinancing conditions.

5. AI CAPITAL EXPENDITURE CYCLE

The AI investment cycle continues to reshape capital allocation.

Current beneficiaries include:

  • semiconductor manufacturing
  • networking infrastructure
  • electrical equipment
  • power generation
  • cooling systems
  • data centers

Long-duration AI investment remains supportive for infrastructure providers while compressing near-term free cash flow for hyperscale technology companies.

The central investment question is no longer:

"How much are companies spending?"

Instead it has become:

"Will future returns exceed the cost of capital?"

CIO CONCLUSION

Markets continue to transition from an era defined by abundant liquidity to one governed by capital efficiency. Elevated financing costs are reshaping investment decisions, increasing the importance of free cash flow, balance sheet resilience, and disciplined capital allocation. While systemic liquidity remains adequate and recession is not our base case, the margin for execution error has narrowed across both public and private markets.

Institutional portfolios should remain diversified, maintain adequate liquidity, and evaluate opportunities through the lens of risk-adjusted return on invested capital rather than headline growth alone. In the current environment, durable cash generation and financial flexibility remain the primary differentiators.
