**Module 7: Behavioral Finance Corner**

**Anchoring Bias:** The tendency for market participants to fixate on historical reference points—such as pre-pandemic zero-interest-rate policy (ZIRP) benchmark yields or peak sub-300 bps credit spreads—when evaluating current risk pricing.

In the current credit cycle, anchoring causes underwriters and risk managers to view high coupon obligations as "temporary aberrations" rather than the permanent baseline, leading to underpriced risk in long-dated credit facilities.

> **Rule of Thumb:** Always run debt sustainability and cash-flow coverage stress tests using the historical 20-year median base rate rather than short-term forward-curve consensus projections.

Keep your risk parameters tight and your tail hedges funded—the next re-pricing event won't wait for your models to update.
