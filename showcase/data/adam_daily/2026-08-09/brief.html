Structural vs. Reduced-Form Synthesis

The methodology behind ADAM v30.2: Bridging Merton models with hazard rate tractability.

The Failure of Pure Diffusion

Classical Merton models treat default as a continuous process. However, empirical data shows "jumps" (e.g., accounting fraud or sudden regulatory shifts) trigger defaults that diffusion paths cannot predict. ADAM v30.2 incorporates Poisson Jump-Diffusion to model these non-linear events.

dAt = (μ - λk)At dt + σAt dWt + At dNt

Reduced-Form Intensity Mapping

Instead of treating default as a firm-value barrier, we treat it as an exogenous surprise. The hazard rate (λ) defines the probability of default in the next infinitesimal time interval. We map deterministic Z-scores to these hazard rates for CDS pricing.

λ = -ln(1 - PD) / t | Spread = λ × LGD × 10,000

XVA & Regulatory Constraints

Modern credit pricing requires Valuation Adjustments (XVA). CVA (Credit), FVA (Funding), and KVA (Capital) reflect the true economic cost of maintaining a position under Basel IV constraints. The "Negative Basis" exists because these costs often exceed the theoretical arbitrage profit.

Non-Markovian Momentum

Ratings drift is not memoryless. Recently downgraded firms enter an "excited state" (Falling Knife Effect), where the probability of a subsequent downgrade is statistically higher than stable peers. Our matrices adjust for this momentum and the global macro state variable (Z).
