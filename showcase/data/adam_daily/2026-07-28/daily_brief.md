Executive Summary
The current macro regime is defined by a Stagflationary Shock, with sticky inflation colliding with decelerating growth metrics. Market actors are aggressively repricing the cost-of-capital as 10-year Treasury yields test the 4.70% threshold, forcing a deleveraging cycle across fundamentally weaker cohorts. This environment has fractured the "soft landing" consensus, exposing structural vulnerabilities in refinancing markets and pushing Loss Given Default (LGD) assumptions materially higher to 0.50.

Our base case points to sustained restrictive policy out of necessity rather than strength, anchoring long-end rates and challenging equity multiples. Primary risks involve nonlinear liquidity events in Private Credit and Commercial Real Estate, where refinancing walls meet punitive interest rates. Specialized agent forecasts indicate a rising probability of idiosyncratic defaults spilling into broader index volatility, demanding a strictly defensive and highly selective allocation strategy favoring pristine credit and hard assets.

5. STAGFLATIONARY SURVIVAL REGIME
The biggest structural trend currently driving capital allocation is the pivot from growth-at-any-cost to cash-flow-duration and tangible asset backing. In a world where nominal growth is high but real growth is negative, capital flows toward entities that can pass on price increases without destroying demand. Beneficiaries include Energy Infrastructure, Defense Contractors, and select Mega-Cap Tech with fortress balance sheets. The central investment question being asked is: *Which balance sheets can absorb sustained 5%+ short-term rates without suffering structural impairment?*
