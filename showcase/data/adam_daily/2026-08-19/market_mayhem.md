## Module 4: BSL Market Update & The Repricing Mirage

The Broadly Syndicated Loan (BSL) market exhibits an illusory strength. Driven by an immense wave of institutional Collateralized Loan Obligation (CLO) creation and technical demand, average single-B clearing spreads have compressed toward **SOFR + 321 bps**.

### The BSL vs. Private Credit Dynamics

1. **Spread Wars:** BSL market aggressiveness has forced Direct Lenders to aggressively lower pricing—often matching BSL spreads or offering synthetic rate caps—to prevent high-quality sponsors from syndicating private debt into the public market.
2. **Covenant Degradation:** To retain market share, private credit providers are increasingly adopting "covenant-lite" documentation, eliminating financial maintenance covenants historically considered the asset class's primary defense line.
3. **The Late-Cycle Pattern:** This dynamic mirrors previous late-cycle behavior (e.g., 2007, 2018), where aggressive repricings reduced debt-service margins for lenders just as underlying macro fundamentals began to deteriorate. Borrowers are successfully lowering interest expenses, but lenders are taking on structural tail-risk without appropriate risk-adjusted compensation.

## Module 7: Behavioral Finance Corner

### The Trap: Anchoring Bias

Credit committees and risk managers frequently suffer from **Anchoring Bias**—the tendency to rely disproportionately on initial reference points (e.g., pre-2022 ultra-low interest rates or recent historical tight credit spreads) when evaluating current borrower risk profiles.

In the current credit cycle, risk teams often anchor to an assumption that base rates will inevitably return to historic lows, leading them to underwrite 5-year debt structures with overly optimistic interest coverage forecasts.

> **Risk Manager's Rule of Thumb:** *Never underwrite a floating-rate capital structure assuming rate relief; if a borrower cannot maintain an Interest Coverage Ratio above 1.25x at current spot rates plus a 100 bps stress buffer, the loan is mispriced regardless of historical performance.*
